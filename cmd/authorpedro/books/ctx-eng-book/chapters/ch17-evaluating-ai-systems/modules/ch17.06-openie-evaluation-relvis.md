# OpenIE Evaluation (RelVis)

## Beat: Standardized Evaluation for Open Information Extraction

Open Information Extraction (OpenIE) extracts relations without predefined schemas—it finds subject-predicate-object triples and more complex n-ary relations directly from text. The challenge: how do you evaluate extraction quality when there's no schema to compare against?

This is where RelVis matters: it's a toolkit and benchmark for evaluating OpenIE systems, providing standardized datasets and metrics.

## What Is RelVis?

**Paper**: "Analysing Errors of Open Information Extraction Systems"[^relvis]
Presented at Building Linguistically Generalizable NLP Systems at EMNLP 2017

### The Benchmark

RelVis provides:
- **4 datasets**: 3 from news domain + 1 from Wikipedia
- **4,522 labeled sentences**
- **11,243 binary or n-ary OIE relations**

Used to evaluate four OIE systems:
- ClausIE
- OpenIE 4.2
- Stanford OpenIE
- PredPatt

Also analyzed impact of five common error classes on 749 n-ary tuples.

### Key Metrics

RelVis measures:
- **Precision**: How many extracted tuples are correct?
- **Recall**: How many correct tuples were extracted?
- **F1**: Harmonic mean of precision and recall
- **Error analysis**: Which error classes most affect quality?

## Error Classes in OpenIE

The benchmark identifies common error types:
1. **Missing arguments**: Relation extracted but important argument missing
2. **Wrong arguments**: Arguments present but incorrect
3. **Wrong relation**: Extracted relation doesn't match ground truth
4. **Granularity issues**: Relations too coarse or too fine
5. **Noise**: Extracting relations that don't exist in the text

## Relevance to Context Engineering

For context engineering, OpenIE evaluation matters because:

1. **Extraction quality**: If you're extracting relations from context for knowledge graphs, RelVis provides benchmarks
2. **Error analysis**: Understanding error classes helps design better extraction prompts
3. **System comparison**: Compare your pipeline against known OpenIE systems

## Limitations

- **Schema-agnostic means noisy**: OpenIE without ontology = lots of tuples, little usable structure
- **Domain-specific**: Benchmark is news/Wikipedia focused—your domain may differ
- **Not production-ready**: Research benchmark, not enterprise evaluation tool

## State of the Stack

- **OpenIE Systems**: ClausIE, OpenIE 4.2, Stanford OpenIE, PredPatt—all research prototypes
- **RelVis**: Toolkit available but not actively maintained
- **Modern approach**: LLMs can do OpenIE via prompting, but no standard eval benchmark

## Questions to Ask Before Adoption

1. Do you need schema-less extraction, or would ontology-guided extraction work better?
2. Is your domain similar to news/Wikipedia, or do you need custom evaluation?
3. Can you use error class analysis to improve your extraction prompts?
