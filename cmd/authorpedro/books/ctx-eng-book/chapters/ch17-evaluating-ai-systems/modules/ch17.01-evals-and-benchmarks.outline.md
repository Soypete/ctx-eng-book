# Evals and Benchmarks

## Argument
Evals are the mechanism for proving improvement. Benchmarks measure capability, but your own evals measure reliability in your specific system.

## Why it matters (reliability goal)
Tie to Pragmatics pillar: alignment between model behavior and system requirements. Without evals, you can't prove your context engineering is working.

## Beats

1. **The Reliability Question** — How do we prove improvement?
   - AI outputs are probabilistic, not deterministic
   - Test that passed last week might fail this week without code changes
   - This changes everything about test suites, reliability measurement, and proving improvement

2. **Evals as Automated Judgment** — evidence: ledger (Agent Evals Need Harnesses)
   - LLM-as-judge: another AI evaluates output quality
   - Golden datasets: inputs with known-correct outputs for regression
   - Judges have biases—golden datasets catch judge failures

3. **Benchmarks—When to Use, When to Ignore** — evidence: existing prose
   - Benchmarks measure capability, not your system's reliability
   - Use for model selection, ignore for design decisions
   - Benchmark is a signal, not a target

## Conclusion to reach
The reader should believe that evals are the answer to "how do we prove improvement?"—but only if built correctly with golden datasets, not just benchmark chasing.
