# Extraction Method Selection: Rule-Based vs LLM

## The Selection Framework

Not every extraction task needs an LLM. The method selection should be driven by task complexity, volume, and reliability requirements.

## Rule-Based Extraction

**When to use:**
- Fixed-format data (tables, forms, structured APIs)
- High-volume, stable-schema documents
- Deterministic requirements where every extraction must be exact
- Cost-sensitive at scale

**Cost profile:**
- Upfront: High (rule development, testing)
- Runtime: Low (simple string/regex operations)
- Scaling: Nearly free at high volume

## LLM Extraction

**When to use:**
- Unstructured or semi-structured text
- Variable formats requiring understanding
- Novel domain exploration
- Tasks where "good enough" is acceptable

**Cost profile:**
- Upfront: Low (prompt engineering)
- Runtime: Per-token API costs
- Scaling: Linear with volume

## Hybrid Approach: The Pragmatic Middle

The most cost-effective extraction pipelines often combine both:

1. **Rule-based pre-processing**: Extract obvious, structured elements with regex or templates
2. **LLM for ambiguous content**: Use LLMs only where rules fail or where context matters
3. **Verification layer**: Run LLM outputs through rule-based validators

## The Efficiency Principle

> You don't need perfect extraction — you need good enough extraction with good guardrails. [Research Ledger: knowledge-graphs/ie-ner.md]

The selection isn't about choosing the "best" method. It's about matching the method to the cost-reliability tradeoff each component of your pipeline requires.
