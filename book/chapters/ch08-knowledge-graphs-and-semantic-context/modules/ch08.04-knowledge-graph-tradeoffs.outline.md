# Knowledge Graph Tradeoffs

## Argument

A graph earns its operational cost only when relationship-centric queries and invariants improve a measured reliability outcome more than simpler source-backed interfaces.

## Reliability goal

Prevent teams from adding stale derived state and semantic operating burden to problems better solved with existing databases, search, tools, or policy code.

## Sequence

1. Establish graph adoption as an architectural commitment.
2. Begin with competency questions and existing interfaces.
3. Identify signals for and against graph adoption.
4. Govern cross-domain semantics without assuming one universal or many isolated graphs.
5. Separate traversal, inference, validation, and algorithms.
6. Count lifecycle and organizational cost.
7. Run a reversible, source-backed pilot with outcome measures.

## Evidence

- [Allemang & Sequeda, 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.11706) supports ontology-based checking in its text-to-SPARQL benchmark, not a universal reasoner requirement.
- Repository memstore synthesis supports learning the domain through simple source-backed tools before investing in richer relationship models.

## Corrections from draft

- Graphs are not inevitable, and tool/business semantics are not merely stopgaps.
- A labeled graph remains useful without formal reasoning; ontology presence does not prove truth.
- Topical ownership does not require physically disconnected graphs.
- Dijkstra and strongly connected components are not automatically context optimizers or bounded domains.
- Unsupported vendor/wiki comparisons and multilingual duplication removed.
