# Schemas, Taxonomies, and Ontologies

## Argument

Semantic models turn domain meaning into an engineering interface, but explicit structure improves reliability only when identity, authority, temporal semantics, validation, and lifecycle are governed.

## Reliability goal

Narrow model interpretation by making decision-relevant vocabulary and relationships queryable and testable without claiming that graph structure guarantees truth.

## Sequence

1. Contrast language inference with an application contract.
2. Separate schemas, taxonomies, and ontologies.
3. Establish identity and relationship definitions before traversal.
4. Show when an assertion needs more structure than a simple triple.
5. Separate description, inference, and validation semantics.
6. Model from competency questions and invariants.
7. State the limits of explicit structure.

## Architecture example

A supply agreement preserves contract, region, effective time, and source that a flat `vendor supplies product` edge loses.

## Corrections from draft

- Explicit graph relationships are not automatically correct, deterministic, constrained, or unambiguous.
- RDF/OWL reasoning is not closed-world data validation.
- Reasoning need not shift wholesale from graph to model, and model interpretation is not inherently more reliable.
- Vendor benchmarks, product survey, public-ontology bootstrapping prescription, and unsupported tool/LoRA progression removed.
