# Guardrails for Extraction Validation

## Accepted Is Not the Same as True

Extracted structure is a proposal. Validation can establish that the proposal has the right shape, uses supported terms, preserves required evidence, and does not violate known application invariants. It cannot establish that every accepted statement is true.

That narrower contract is still valuable. It prevents malformed, unsupported, contradictory, or unauthorized proposals from flowing directly into a graph that later retrieval treats as authoritative.

## Validate in Layers

1. **Structural validation:** required fields, types, identifiers, units, and serialization.
2. **Semantic validation:** allowed classes, relationship direction, cardinality under the application's closed-world policy, and temporal rules.
3. **Source validation:** passage locator exists and supports the proposed statement, including negation, modality, and attribution.
4. **Identity validation:** endpoints resolve under the active entity policy without crossing tenant scope.
5. **Authority validation:** the source class is permitted to support this kind of assertion.
6. **Conflict handling:** supersession or coexistence follows a declared temporal and authority rule.
7. **Promotion policy:** accept, reject, quarantine, or request review according to consequence and uncertainty.

Keep ontology entailment distinct from operational checks. A domain/range axiom may infer a type under RDFS semantics; a SHACL shape or application rule may reject an ingestion record. Use the mechanism whose behavior matches the boundary.

## Do Not Trust a Single Confidence Number

Language-model self-reported confidence is not a calibrated probability by default. Source quality, extractor score, agreement, and validation results describe different uncertainties and should remain separate features.

Set promotion thresholds on a labeled validation set and review them by assertion type. High confidence should not auto-accept a relationship with a severe false-positive cost. Some policy, identity, and authorization assertions should require an authoritative source or explicit approval regardless of score.

## Preserve the Decision Record

For each proposal, retain source and span, extractor and contract versions, normalized candidate, identity resolution, validation results, reviewer or policy decision, timestamps, and supersession state. Store rejected high-risk proposals when policy permits so recurring failures can be analyzed without allowing them into normal retrieval.

Test every validator with counterexamples, then test the chain. Include a well-shaped false claim, negated relation, ambiguous identity, stale source, unauthorized tenant, conflicting authority, duplicate delivery, and ontology migration. Verify rejection, review routing, deletion, and replay.

Measure false acceptance, false rejection, review yield, validator escape rate, correction latency, provenance coverage, and downstream use of unapproved state. A graph containing “everything validated” still overclaims. The reliable graph contains assertions accepted under named policies, with evidence and limitations that retrieval preserves.

The final module applies the whole pipeline to multilingual sources, where aggregate accuracy hides the most variation.
