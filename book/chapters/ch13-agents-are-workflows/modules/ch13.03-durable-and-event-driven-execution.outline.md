# Durable and Event-Driven Execution

## Argument
Durable execution converts crashes, duplicate delivery, and uncertain side effects into known workflow states with safe next actions.

## Why it matters (reliability goal)
Reliability depends on checkpoint semantics, idempotency, reconciliation, ordering, and a precise definition of replay.

## Beats

1. **Durable Execution** — evidence: distributed-systems patterns
   - Checkpoint workflow, version, evidence, effect, authorization, and budget state
   - Reconcile ambiguous effects before retry
   - Transactions, outboxes, event logs, queues, idempotency, and sagas solve different problems

2. **Event-Driven Agent Systems** — evidence: existing prose
   - Pattern: event arrives → agent initializes → executes → produces output
   - Handle identifiers, schema evolution, duplicate/out-of-order delivery, concurrency, and backpressure

3. **Replay Semantics**
   - Historical reconstruction, pure-logic replay, and current-condition rerun are distinct
   - Diagnostic replay must not repeat production effects

## Conclusion to reach
The reader should design recovery around known outcomes, ambiguous outcomes, and safe actions rather than promising seamless restart.
