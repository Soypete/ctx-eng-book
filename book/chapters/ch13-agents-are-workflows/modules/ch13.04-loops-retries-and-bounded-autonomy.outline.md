# Loops, Retries, and Bounded Autonomy

## Argument
Loops amplify both useful feedback and prior errors in agents and other iterative workflows. Bounded autonomy requires observable progress, classified failures, and software-owned terminal policy.

## Why it matters (reliability goal)
Assign decisions according to the invariant and consequence, not merely whether they appear easy or hard.

## Beats

1. **The Decision Boundary** — evidence: existing prose
   - Models propose evidence, tools, plan revisions, and completion; deterministic workers may produce equivalent proposals in non-agent workflows
   - Software validates scope, authority, state, effects, and terminal outcomes

2. **Loops and Retries** — evidence: existing prose
   - Task-specific progress, budgets, terminal outcomes, and structured context
   - Retry transient safe operations; reconcile ambiguous effects
   - Circuit breakers, jitter, backpressure, and global retry budgets

3. **Bounded Autonomy** — evidence: existing prose
   - Add typed output, tools, durable state, retrieval, authorization, and loops only as required
   - Instrument each new boundary before expanding autonomy

## Conclusion to reach
The reader should expand model discretion one enforced and measured boundary at a time, while keeping deterministic workflow state, authorization, validation, retries, and execution outside the model.
