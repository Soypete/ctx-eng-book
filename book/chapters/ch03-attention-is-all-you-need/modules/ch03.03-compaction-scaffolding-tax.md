# Compaction, Attention, and the Scaffolding Tax

## The Systems Perspective

Here's what matters for building reliable AI systems:

1. **You control the assembled input.** What you include, exclude, order, and compress constrains the output, although model capability and decoding still matter.

2. **More input is not automatically better input.** Distractors can degrade performance for some models and tasks, while missing evidence guarantees that evidence cannot be used. Context selection is an evaluated tradeoff, not a universal attention-dilution formula.

3. **Context windows have hard interface limits.** There is no infinite invocation memory. Decide what is omitted, summarized, or retrieved later before a provider's truncation behavior decides implicitly.

4. **Cost is not optional.** Whether you're paying per token or running on your own GPUs, context has real costs. Engineering for cost means being intentional about what gets included, not just maximizing token counts.

5. **Position matters.** The model reasons about sequence. Poorly organized context degrades even correct information because attention can't establish the right relationships.

The transformer architecture gave us a powerful way to combine information within an input. It does not decide whether the application assembled the correct evidence, preserved an exception during summarization, or applied the right authorization policy.

I first heard the phrase **scaffolding tax** from [Saul Ramirez](https://www.linkedin.com/in/saulramirez/) during a Utah meetup talk about the language-model work his team was doing at Subquadratic. I use it here for the latency, tokens, state, code, and additional failure modes introduced by orchestration around a model. This attribution comes from my recollection of the talk; it is not a claim that the phrase originated in a published paper or that Saul coined it.

The connection to Subquadratic's work is architectural. The company describes retrieval pipelines, chunking strategies, and agentic scaffolding as useful workarounds for the cost and context limits of dense-attention models, and is developing long-context models around subquadratic sparse attention [Subquadratic, 2026](https://subq.ai/subq-1-1-small-technical-report). That is the company's account of its design and results, not independent evidence that model architecture eliminates retrieval, authorization, state, or evaluation work.

Some scaffolding is essential: authentication, authorization, tool execution, observability, and durable workflows are system responsibilities. Other scaffolding compensates for a particular model's context or orchestration limitations. A model that can process a complete artifact may reduce chunking, retrieval, or multi-agent coordination for some tasks. It does not make source authority, permissions, durable effects, or outcome evaluation disappear.

Summarization is the clearest example. A summary is produced for some anticipated use. If the system discards the source, information omitted by that summary is unavailable to later invocations. The loss is irreversible only relative to the retained representation; keeping source records and provenance makes a later rehydration possible.

That is both a context-selection problem and a state-lifecycle decision. The system must decide what remains authoritative, what may be regenerated, and what a summary is allowed to replace.

---

**Reliability Question Answered:** The model can only use input supplied or retrieved during the workflow. Compaction changes that available evidence, so it must be observable, reversible where required, and tested against future task classes.

## Choose a Compaction Policy

There is no single compaction strategy. Choose based on what later work must recover:

1. **Deterministic filtering** removes known boilerplate, duplicate passages, and fields outside the task contract. It is cheap and testable, but only works when the removal rule is explicit.
2. **Extractive compaction** retains selected source spans. It preserves exact wording and provenance, but may omit relationships expressed across spans.
3. **Abstractive summarization** produces a smaller synthesis. It can preserve an argument better than isolated excerpts, but may flatten uncertainty, chronology, exceptions, or attribution.
4. **Hierarchical retrieval** keeps summaries as routing aids while retaining the source beneath them. The first request retrieves a compact node; later steps expand only the branches needed for the task.
5. **Structured state** moves durable facts, decisions, and workflow transitions into typed records instead of repeatedly summarizing conversation text.

The naive agent appends every message and periodically asks the model to “summarize everything so far.” A more reliable iteration separates the transcript from durable state, records the compaction policy and source IDs, and retrieves original evidence when a decision depends on detail. The summary becomes a cache or index—not the new source of truth.

## Test What Compaction Loses

A smaller prompt is not automatically an improvement. Evaluate compaction with scenarios that depend on details most summaries tend to erase:

- an exception to a general rule
- the order of two events
- who asserted a disputed fact
- a revoked permission
- an unresolved question
- a failed tool attempt that must not be repeated

For each policy, compare task success against an uncompacted baseline. Track token reduction, latency, source-recovery rate, contradiction rate, and the percentage of outputs whose claims retain provenance. Inject a known exception before compaction and verify that the later workflow can still recover it. This is failure injection for context state.

## Pay Scaffolding Deliberately

The goal is not to eliminate scaffolding. It is to know which layer earns its cost. Authentication and replay may add latency while making the workflow recoverable and safe. A three-agent debate may add latency without improving the task. Keep a layer when controlled evaluation shows that it improves reliability enough to justify its operational cost.

The practical rule is simple: retain authoritative source data, treat generated summaries as derived artifacts, attach provenance and policy versions, and make rehydration possible when the task requires details the compact form omitted.
