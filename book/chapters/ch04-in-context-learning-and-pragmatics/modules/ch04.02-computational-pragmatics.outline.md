# Computational Pragmatics

## Chapter-spine placement
Pragmatics is the purpose, actor, referent, and permissible consequence of a
language process. It follows the **context engineering framework**: available
sources and authorities, their domain meaning and relationships, then what may
be said or done with that meaning. Prompts and agent loops are techniques that
can expose or enforce pragmatic requirements, not the definition of the layer.

## Argument
Speech-act and reference-resolution failures occur when a system has information but has not established the intended action, referent, or scope.

## Why it matters (reliability goal)
Align answers, extractions, classifications, recommendations, workflow steps,
and actions with purpose and scope. Without explicit pragmatic context
(goals, constraints, actor, and referent), even correct semantic information
can produce the wrong outcome.

## Beats

1. **Speech Act Interpretation** — evidence: [Jurafsky computational pragmatics](research/computational-pragmatics-notes.md), optional [ReAct paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03629)
   - Four inferential problems: reference resolution, speech act, discourse coherence, abduction
   - Do not rank speech-act interpretation without production evidence
   - ReAct, chain-of-thought, and planning structure computation; they do not establish user intent

2. **"Doing the Inference Work"** — evidence: optional [DSPy example](research/dspy-notes.md)
   - Instead of vague prompts, explicitly specify desired outcome and constraints
   - DSPy is an optional example of formalizing: "do this, and I want this result"

3. **Indexicality Problem** — evidence: [Pragmatics as bridge](research/wordnet-anchor-text-notes.md)
   - "The data", "my files" — same phrase, different referents in different contexts
   - Without explicit scoping → wrong data access → plausible but incorrect answers

## Conclusion to reach
The reader should resolve intended action and indexical references before retrieval or execution, using authenticated state, clarification, and policy gates rather than structured guessing.
