# Fine-Tuning and LoRA

Fine-tuning changes model behavior by updating parameters from training examples. It can reduce repeated instruction overhead or improve a stable task behavior, but it also moves policy into a harder-to-inspect artifact. Retrieval, authorization, validation, and durable workflow controls do not disappear.

## Choose the Adaptation Surface

Full fine-tuning updates the model parameters selected by the training procedure. LoRA freezes pretrained weights and injects trainable low-rank matrices into selected layers [Hu et al., 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.09685). In the paper’s evaluated settings, LoRA substantially reduced trainable parameters and training memory while matching or exceeding selected full-fine-tuning baselines. Those numbers are results for particular models, optimizers, and tasks—not a universal multiplier for every deployment.

| Choice | Potential advantage | Reliability cost |
|---|---|---|
| Full fine-tuning | broad adaptation capacity | larger training/deployment artifact and regression surface |
| LoRA or related PEFT | smaller task-specific update; base can remain fixed | adapter composition, serving compatibility, and route/version management |
| No training | fastest rollback and simplest provenance | repeated context cost or unresolved capability gap |

LoRA is not automatically lower quality or lower risk. Rank, target modules, data, optimization, quantization, and base model all matter. Multiple adapters can also conflict or create operational complexity. Benchmark the exact configuration.

## Build Training Data From Diagnosed Behavior

There is no defensible universal count of examples for full fine-tuning or LoRA. Required data depends on diversity, task entropy, base capability, output contract, and acceptable error. Start with a versioned specification and examples whose provenance and rights permit training.

Separate training, development, and held-out test sets by the unit that can leak—customer, document family, time period, or template—not merely random rows. Include negative cases, abstentions, policy boundaries, rare slices, and adversarial inputs. Deduplicate against evaluation data and record how synthetic examples were generated and reviewed.

## Evaluate the System, Not Just the Adapter

Compare the adapted and base systems under identical context and tool policies. Measure target-task outcomes, calibration or abstention where applicable, invalid tool proposals, safety and authorization violations, latency, cost, and regressions on capabilities the application still needs. Standard benchmarks can provide context but cannot replace the production distribution.

Deploy through a versioned route with shadow traffic or a bounded canary. Retain the base route for rollback. A training win that disappears after retrieval, quantization, or serving changes is not a production win.

Fine-tuning is appropriate when the desired behavior is stable enough to encode, examples represent it, and measured benefit exceeds training and maintenance cost. It is not a way to avoid specifying the task. The specification created through context engineering becomes the training and evaluation contract.
